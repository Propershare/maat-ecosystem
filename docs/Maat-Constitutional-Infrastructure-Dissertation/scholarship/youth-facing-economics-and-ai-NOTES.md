## 1. What this is about (one page)

You are living inside a **change in how wealth is made and who gets paid**.

- **Feudal times:** land was the main wealth; lords owned land; peasants worked it.  
- **Capitalist times (roughly last few centuries):** factories, machines, banks, and **wage jobs** became central. Most people sell their **labor** for a paycheck, then buy goods with that paycheck.  
- **Now:** AI, robots, software, and automation can do work that humans used to do — not in one factory, but across many fields at once.

**The big question:** Are we in a normal bad patch (recession, layoffs, then jobs come back)? Or are we at the edge of a **new kind of society** where wage labor is no longer the main way most people survive?

That question is **research**, not a TikTok slogan.

## 2. Five ideas you must understand

### Idea A — Different stages, different rules

A seed and a full tree both follow biology, but the **rules for a seed** are not the same as for a mature tree.

Same with societies:

| Stage (simplified) | Main wealth | Main conflict (simplified) |
|------------------|-------------|----------------------------|
| Feudal | Land | Lords vs peasants |
| Capitalist | Capital + wage labor | Owners vs workers |
| (debated next stage) | ? | ? |

**Do not** use feudal rules to explain TikTok economics. **Do not** assume capitalism lasts forever because it is all you have seen.

### Idea B — The wage cycle (why your paycheck matters to the whole system)

In classic capitalism, businesses need this loop:

1. Hire workers  
2. Pay wages  
3. Workers buy products  
4. Businesses make profit  
5. Reinvest  
6. Repeat  

Workers are **both** the people who make things **and** the people who buy things.

If step 2 shrinks for millions of people, step 3 breaks — even if robots make **more** stuff than ever.

### Idea C — Cyclical crisis vs structural crisis

| Type | What it looks like | Examples |
|------|-------------------|----------|
| **Cyclical** | Boom → bust → recovery; jobs return in some form | 2008 recession, COVID shock |
| **Structural** | The **basic setup** of the economy stops working | Feudal land system vs rising trade; *possibly* mass automation vs wage-labor system |

The essay argues AI might push us toward **structural** pressure. **Your job:** find evidence for or against — don't repeat it because an elder said so.

### Idea D — Ruling groups rarely walk away quietly

When a system is under deep stress, those who benefit most from the old system usually **fight to keep it** — with laws, propaganda, police, prisons, and violence. History shows minorities are often **blamed first**.

That is not pessimism for its own sake. It is a pattern you can study (Weimar Germany, Red Summer, mass incarceration, immigration scapegoating).

### Idea E — Truth over performance

If adults give you slogans without study — "learn to code," " hustle," "AI will save us," "vote harder" — you will eventually see the gap.

**Better:** Learn **how** to read evidence: who owns the robots, who owns the data centers, who writes the laws, who gets arrested when factories close.

## 3. What the essay claims (in plain language)

1. Capitalism has its own "engine" (profit, competition, wage labor).  
2. AI and automation may break that engine by removing wages while production stays high.  
3. That is deeper than a recession.  
4. Black communities and other oppressed groups are especially exposed — historically first, structurally now.  
5. In the US, racism has often split working people against each other; unity is hard and cannot be assumed.  
6. Youth need **serious** political economy — not kindergarten finance literacy.

**What the essay does not fully prove:** exactly **when** this happens, or that fascism is **inevitable**. Those are hypotheses to test.

## 5. Study questions (for class or self-study)

1. Give one example where technology **destroyed** jobs and one where it **created** new ones. Same era, different sectors.  
2. Explain cyclical vs structural crisis in your own words, with one historical example each.  
3. Why would a factory that doubles output with fewer workers still **lose** if nobody can afford to buy?  
4. What is "labor power" in Marx's sense? Why is it special compared to other commodities?  
5. Name one way ruling classes tried to **patch** capitalism in crisis (war spending, credit, welfare, new markets). Did it work forever?  
6. How does racism affect **class solidarity** in US history? Give one positive and one negative example.
