## A. Essay move → readings

| Essay section / claim | Read first | Read next (depth) | Notes |
|----------------------|------------|-------------------|--------|
| Every society has its own "rules of motion"; stages differ | Marx, *Preface to A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy* (1859) | Marx, *Grundrisse* (1857–58), notebooks on "forms preceding capitalist production" | Core historical-materialist method |
| Feudal → capitalist transition; land vs wage labor | Marx, *Capital* Vol. I, Part VIII (primitive accumulation) | Polanyi, *The Great Transformation* (1944); Brenner debate essays (Aston & Philpin) | Essay's feudal/capital contrast |
| Capitalism not eternal; relations of production can be outgrown | Marx, *Capital* Vol. III (tendency of rate of profit); Engels, *Socialism: Utopian and Scientific* | Arrighi, *The Long Twentieth Century* (1994); Wallerstein, world-systems summaries | "Engine changes" argument |
| Cyclical vs **structural** crisis | Marx, *Capital* Vol. I, Ch. 3 (money/credit); Vol. III, crisis theory fragments | Grossman/Luxemburg accumulation debates; Harvey, *The Limits to Capital* (1982) | Essay's key distinction |
| Underconsumption: produce more, buy less | Luxemburg, *The Accumulation of Capital* (1913) — Ch. 1–3 | Baran & Sweezy, *Monopoly Capital* (1966); Kalecki on effective demand | "Who buys the products?" |
| Wage labor as producer **and** consumer of commodities | Marx, *Capital* Vol. I, Ch. 4–6 (labor power, surplus value) | Braverman, *Labor and Monopoly Capital* (1974) | Essay's profit cycle (steps 1–6) |
| AI/robotics as **labor-replacing** across sectors | Frey & Osborne, "The Future of Employment" (2013) — **as foil** | Autor, "Why Are There Still So Many Jobs?" (2015); Acemoglu & Restrepo, "Tasks, Automation, and Rising Inequality" (2018+) | Test "qualitatively different" claim |
| Value if labor is no longer central | Marx, *Capital* Vol. I, Ch. 1 (value theory) | Srnicek & Williams, *Inventing the Future* (2015); Mazzucato, *The Value of Everything* (2018) | Open theoretical problem |
| Ruling classes resist; repression increases | Marx & Engels, *Manifesto*; Lenin, *State and Revolution* (1917) | Neumann, *Behemoth* (1942) on Nazi political economy; Paxton, *Anatomy of Fascism* (2004) | Essay's counterrevolution thesis — treat as **hypothesis** |
| Scapegoating minorities in crisis | Arendt, *Origins of Totalitarianism* (1951) — Ch. on race/imperialism | Gilmore, *Golden Gulag* (2007); Alexander, *The New Jim Crow* (2010) | Strong empirical support |
| US racial division blocks class unity | Du Bois, *Black Reconstruction* (1935) | Robinson, *Black Marxism* (1983); Roediger, *Wages of Whiteness* (1991); Taylor, *Race for Profit* (2019) | Nuance essay's "white working class" claim |
| Black populations first-targeted in transition | Du Bois; Robinson | Davis, *Prisoners of the American Dream*; Kelley, *Freedom Dreams* | Connect to carceral/surveillance economy |
| "Tell youth only researched truth" | Freire, *Pedagogy of the Oppressed* (1968) | hooks, *Teaching to Transgress* (1994); UKMT pedagogical texts below | Ethical core of essay |

## C. Automation & AI — empirical shelf (verify, don't assume)

| Work | Why read it |
|------|-------------|
| Brynjolfsson & McAfee, *The Second Machine Age* (2014) | Mainstream "technology complements labor" case |
| OECD (2023+) AI and labour market reports | Policy-level employment projections |
| ILR / BLS occupational outlook + displacement studies | US sector data |
| EPI inequality reports (Mishel, Gould) | Purchasing power / wage stagnation evidence |
| Zuboff, *Surveillance Capitalism* (2019) | Where profit goes when labor is data |
| Crawford, *Atlas of AI* (2021) | Material base: energy, land, extraction, Global South |
| Noble, *Algorithms of Oppression* (2018) | Race/gender in automated systems |

**Research question for youth (from essay):** Is current AI deployment **cyclical** (recession, glut, recovery) or **structural** (wage labor ceasing to organize economic life)? Evidence required before conclusion.
