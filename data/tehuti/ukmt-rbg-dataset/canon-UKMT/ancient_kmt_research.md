**SYSTEMATIC RESEARCH FRAMEWORK FOR THE STUDY OF ANCIENT KMT**

| Stage | Ancient Kmtian Term (Transliteration) | Meaning / Role in Kmtian Thought | Purpose | Key Activities | Risks if Omitted | Illustrative Examples |
|-------|----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|---------|----------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Problem Statement | Hwt Hsb (House of Accounting) | The place where problems and questions are formulated | Define the central research question about Kmt’s birth, development, decline, and death | State the historical problem clearly (e.g., causes of collapse of Old Kingdom); Identify scope (chronological & thematic) | Research drifts into speculation without clear focus | Asking “Why did the Old Kingdom collapse?” instead of vague “Why did Kmt fall?” |
| Review of Literature | Medu Neter (Words of the Gods) | Refers to sacred inscriptions, texts, and records | Survey prior interpretations, both ancient and modern | Review classical sources (Herodotus, Manetho); Review modern archaeology, African-centered revisions (Finch, Diop) | Risk of recycling old Eurocentric biases or myths | Comparing Herodotus’s claims with archaeological layers of the Nile Valley |
| Theoretical Framework | Maat (Truth, Balance, Order) | Guiding principle of justice and cosmic order | Ground research in a clear interpretive framework | Apply birth-death-rebirth (KA Method); Integrate environmental, social, and political lenses | Risk of fragmented or biased conclusions without coherent theory | Using Maat to balance environmental and social explanations of collapse |
| Research Design | Sesh (Writing, Planning) | The act of designing and recording knowledge | Plan systematic steps to investigate Kmt’s life cycle | Define chronological units (Early Dynastic → Late Period); Define institutions (state, economy, religion) | Research becomes piecemeal, not replicable | Dividing Old, Middle, New Kingdoms into comparable units |
| Methods of Research | Per-ankh (House of Life) | Temple school for knowledge, medicine, and archives | Establish methodological tools for inquiry | - Archaeology - Textual criticism - Comparative anthropology | Using only one method creates one-dimensional history | Using only pyramids (archaeology) but ignoring papyri (textual) |
| Identification of Evidence | Khet (Thing, Object) | Refers to material reality, artifacts | Identify categories of relevant evidence | - Artifacts, tombs, temples - Texts (Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts) - Environmental records (Nile flood layers) | Risk of missing whole dimensions of history | Ignoring climate data when studying Old Kingdom decline |
| Collection & Ordering of Evidence | Sedjeru (To arrange, put in order) | Kmtian concept of ordering/arranging things properly | Organize evidence into coherent categories | - Stratigraphy for artifacts - Chronological ordering - Thematic ordering by institution | Chaos of data, inability to trace cause-effect | Ordering flood records alongside dynastic succession |
| Verification of Evidence | Maat Henu (True of Voice, Justified) | Term used for verified truth, often in afterlife judgments | Ensure authenticity of evidence | - Stratigraphic checks - Radiocarbon dating - Cross-comparison of inscriptions - Detection of forgeries | - False dynastic timelines - Reliance on propaganda texts - Acceptance of fakes | Verifying Tutankhamun’s CT scans to test accident theories |
| Detailed Analysis | Rekh (Knowledge, Understanding) | Kmtian word for knowing/understanding | Examine evidence to uncover patterns & causes | - Comparative chronology (Mesopotamia, Nubia, Hittites) - Nile flood & climate analysis - Military campaigns & governance records - Social & religious shifts | Oversimplified causes (e.g., invasions only); Misreading cultural change as abrupt | Analyzing Late Period decline using both foreign invasions & internal corruption |
| Synthesis of Conclusions | Djed (Stability, Enduring Order) | Symbol of integration, continuity, and stability | Build coherent historical narrative | - Construct unified timeline - Link multiple lenses (environment + politics + society) - Compare Kmt’s trajectory with other civilizations - Debate competing theories | Fragmented dynastic histories without continuity; Overgeneralizations (e.g., “Kmt fell because of Sea Peoples”) | Explaining New Kingdom collapse as convergence of drought, invasions, bureaucracy, and trade loss |
| Interdependence of Stages | Ka (Vital Essence, Life Force) | The binding principle of vitality & interconnection | Demonstrate how stages reinforce one another | - Verification grounds analysis - Analysis reveals causes - Synthesis creates meaning | - Without verification: myths & propaganda dominate - Without analysis: neglect of systemic shifts - Without synthesis: disconnected “facts” | Understanding pyramids via e
