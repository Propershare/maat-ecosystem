Title: Enslavement Process (Arab Enslavement → European Enslavement → Colonialism → Neocolonialism)

| Phase/Period | Process / Actions | Mechanisms of Violence & Control | Consequences |
|--------------|--------------------|----------------------------------|--------------|
| Early Arab Enslavement (7th c.) | Scouts infiltrate villages; map vulnerable routes; merchants finance armed raids | Surprise attacks at dawn; burning of homes; mass abductions; killing resisters | Depopulation of Sahel, Nile Valley, E. Africa |
| Arab Raids | Raiding parties surround settlements; captives selected by age & gender | Men killed; boys earmarked for castration; women seized for concubinage | Severe demographic losses; population imbalance |
| Slave Boy Castration | Boys taken to slave markets or desert camps; partial or total castration | Mortality rates up to 70–85%; forced religious conversion; isolation | Lineages extinguished; assimilation into Arab-Islamic identity |
| Sale of Women to Harems | Women & children assigned to harems; sold across Red Sea & Persian Gulf | Rape; forced pregnancy; total domestic confinement | African genetic absorption into Arab societies without heritage rights |
| Forced Marches Across Sahara | Captives marched 300–2,000 miles to desert ports | Starvation, thirst, beatings, death from exhaustion; abandonment of sick | Massive mortality during transit; loss of ethnic memory |
| Slave Ports | Distribution centers: Zanzibar, Mogadishu, Suakin, Cairo | Branding, further beatings, family separation, new auctions | Ethnic fragmentation and trauma |
| Early European Involvement | Portuguese, Dutch, English, French build forts | Bribe local rulers, arm rival states; intensify wars | Militarization of West Africa; restructure around war economy |
| Initial Captures | Kidnappings, war-captives, judicial enslavement | Iron shackles; village burnings; riverbank ambushes | Ghana → Mali → Songhay, Kongo destabilized |
| Slave Coffles | Hundreds chained together, marched weeks to months | Execution of the weak; suicides; hunger; exposure | 35-50% die before reaching coast |
| Slave Forts | Held in dank, airless chambers (Elmina, Cape Coast, Whydah) | Disease, rape, torture, overcrowding | |
| Middle Passage / Tight Packing | Stowed in cargo holds | Chains, heat, suffocation, dysentery, dehydration | 30–40% die on floating cemeteries |
| Seasoning in Americas | Sold on docks in Charleston, Bahia, Havana, Kingston | Physical inspection, sexual exploitation, forced renaming | Social death; destruction of cultural memory |
| Plantation System | Assigned to sugar, rice, cotton, coffee, tobacco plantations | 15–19 hour days; whipping; mutilation; rape; torture | Life expectancy <7 years; forced generational slavery |
| Maritime Slave Roles | African sailors, soldiers, domestic workers, concubines | Harsh desert transport; forced assimilation | African-descended diaspora disconnected from continent |
| Empire Resistance (1500–1900) | Ghana → Mali → Songhay → Kanem-Bornu react to external pressures | Slave raids weaken states; economic reorientation to exports | Loss of unity and sovereignty |
| Formal Colonial Rule (1885–1960) | European partition & military occupation | Forced labor, taxation, extraction, rail systems built for exports | Destruction of indigenous development models |
| Neocolonialism (1960–) | Debt traps, coups, currency control, mineral concessions | IMF/World Bank austerity; corporate mining; denial of high tech | Continual resource depletion, stunted development cycles |
