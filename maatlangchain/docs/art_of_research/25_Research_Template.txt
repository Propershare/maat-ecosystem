# K2 Methodology  
## Dialectical Development Template

---

### 1. Formation of Unity
The early formation of nascent unity. Internal elements are relatively undifferentiated and still forming (e.g., early relationship formation where similarities, interests, etc., are emphasized).

---

### 2. Strengthening of Unity
Unity continues to form; its central unifying force grows stronger and more certain. The bond between internal elements deepens.

---

### 3. Emergence of Internal Contradictions
The strength of the unity increases and internal forces begin to differentiate. Similarities are overshadowed by differences that are essential to the unity.

---

### 4. Polarization
Contradictory motion—internal, self-motion, auto-dynamic. Similar internal elements begin to gravitate toward one another.

---

### 5. Intensification of Differences
Essential differences become more acute. Similar internal elements begin to gravitate toward polar ends of the unity.

---

### 6–10. Unification of Similar Elements
Each of the similar polarizing internal elements unites with similar ones to form unified opposite within the unity.

- One of the opposites—one aspect of the unity—assumes the leading position or role.
- Identity of opposites is unity—each aspect of the unity is formed from similar elements.
- Each opposite necessarily possesses inner unity and concrete identity.
- Each opposite contains internal conditions that give rise to the other opposite.

---

### 11. Interpenetration of Opposites
The set of opposites responsible for conditions that give rise to the other opposites interact in dynamic interpenetration.

---

### 12. Negation Through Interaction
Opposites simultaneously interpenetrate, posit, and negate one another; this dynamic interaction is the basis of motion.

---

### 13. Struggle and Development
The contradictory aspects of the unity are unified and engaged in struggle with one another simultaneously. Such interaction is the basis of development of the unity (mutual exclusion & mutual negation).

---

### 14–16. Dominance and Expansion
Each aspect of the primary contradiction continues growing and becoming more certain until it reaches the limits of its development.

---

### 17. Necessity of Transformation
The opposite begins to interpenetrate one another; this is the beginning of resolution, where confusion or struggle changes form.

---

### 18. Negation of the Negation
The contradictory opposites continue to mutually penetrate and negate one another, negating the existence of each respective counterpart within its opposite.

---

### 19–20. Leading Aspect Shifts
The interpenetrated opposites continue growth within the same unity; the leading, decisive aspect remains dominant, but tension intensifies.

---

### 21. Crisis of Unity
Opposites continue interpenetrating as they grow in unity & struggle—the leading one maintains its hierarchical position—leading to intensification of struggle.

---

### 22. Paralysis Point
Mature opposites within the unity reach a point of momentary paralysis, characterized by no apparent movement. This is an advanced stage of struggle.

---

### 23. Pre-Revolutionary Phase
As opposites continue interpenetrating & antagonism sharpens, their relationship becomes unstable. Each opposite seeks to overcome the other while preserving the integrity of the unity.

---

### 24. Revolutionary Break
Antagonism develops between contradictory opposites as the struggle reaches its culmination. The previous equilibrium is destroyed.

---

### 25. Collapse of Prior Form
Antagonized between contradictory opposites intensifies. The previously subordinate opposite overcomes the formerly dominant opposite.

---

### 26. Reversal
An inversion occurs where the aspect that was once subordinate becomes decisive and dominant. (e.g., white becomes black; black becomes white).

---

### 27. Motion Beyond Unity
Inverted opposites continue moving in unity & struggle until the old line—threshold of qualitative limit—is reached. Within no further room for unity to be maintained.

---

### 28. Breakdown of Equilibrium
The old unity & struggle is temporarily suspended when the quantitative limits of the unity are reached and the opposite motion overcomes the whole.

---

### 29. Struggle Between New & Old
The struggle between opposites becomes antagonistic. The previous form is broken. A new unity begins forming while the old form dissolves.

---

### 30. Temporary Restoration
The reactionary aspect temporarily regains dominance. The struggle resumes; the strength of the old position determines how long it can resist regression.

---

### 31. Regression or Transformation
If the reactionary aspect regains control long enough, regression occurs. If not, revolutionary change advances.

---

### 32. New Revolutionary Unity
A revolutionary new form is forged, strengthened by intense decay of the old structure against the new. The reactionary aspect wages a last struggle.

---

### 33. Accelerated Development
Antagonistic struggle intensifies and accelerates. Progressive tendencies emerge to dominate conservative ones. Old forms collapse.

---

### 34. Metabolic Movement
Depending on which opposite leads, revolutionary movement accelerates or degeneration intensifies. Metabolic movement is decisive.

---

### 35. Rupture
There is a rupture in unity—either progressive or reactionary—old now begins to shed itself of unity’s residue.

---

### 36. Emergence of New Integrity
Out of the remnants of the intense struggle emerges qualitatively new formation that represents either a revolutionary or regressive new unity.

---

### 37. Lower or Higher Development
The revolutionary new formation moves toward resolution on a lower level or higher level. The development continues.

---

### 38. Consolidation
Suppression occurs in the new formation. The revolutionary aspect consolidates its dominance.

---

### 39. Progressive Regression
Progressive regression shifts into reactionary regression if contradictions are mishandled. Preservation of old elements obstructs development.

---

### 40. New Synthesis
The synthesis of the new revolutionary or devolutionary formation stabilizes itself as a new unity.

---

### 41. Formation of New Unity
The formation of a new unity is established to ensure continued growth on a higher or lower level. Development proceeds.

---

### 42. Continuous Process
The qualitatively new unity continues its course of development through struggle of opposites. The basis is laid for ongoing regressions or advances.

- Related groups → McNemar test

- Two related groups → Sign test / Wilcoxon signed-rank

- Three or more related groups → Friedman two-way ANOVA

### Interval or Ratio Dependent Variable
- Two independent groups → t-test for independent groups
- Two related groups → t-test for related groups
- Three or more independent groups → One-way ANOVA
- Three or more related groups → Form of ANOVA appropriate for data

- Ordinal → Kolmogorov–Smirnov one-sample test
- Interval or Ratio → One-sample t-test

# Focus Group Research  
## Structured Qualitative Research Framework

---

## I. Objective Conditions & Data Decision

### Purpose Test
**Is the purpose of the research to determine the perceptions, feelings, or thoughts of a population regarding products, services, and/or other issues?**

- **NO** → Select another research design  
- **YES** → Continue

---

## II. Identify the Research Problem
- Identify the research problem / interest
- Conduct a preliminary literature review to clearly define the research problem
- Describe the context of the research problem / issue
- Conduct a comprehensive literature review, including:
  - Others who have studied similar issues
  - Relevant population members

---

## III. Population & Site Selection
- Identify population to study
- Carefully select site
- Construct a plan of action considering:
  - Issue under study
  - Target population members & needs
  - Data collection design
  - Data analysis design
  - Information & resource needs
  - Available human & material resources
  - Timeframe

---

## IV. Theoretical Framework & Conceptualization
- Establish conceptual model of relationships between central elements / variables to be observed
- Generate clear & precise conceptual definitions of:
  - Concepts
  - Variables
- Define how concepts & variables will be studied or measured in the target population

---

## V. Instrument & Question Design
- Construct a set of questions and/or themes to cover during the interview
- Refine wording, language, or gestures to be sensitive to participants
- Consider impact of question order
- Clarify concepts that may have multiple meanings

---

## VI. Preparation & Logistics

### Personnel & Training
- Select & appropriately train a moderator and other assistants if necessary

### Location & Pilot
- Secure a location to conduct the focus group(s)
- Conduct a pilot focus group:
  - Select pilot participants with similar characteristics to target population
  - Test questions
  - Test appropriateness of question order & flow
  - Observe effectiveness of moderator

### Revision
- Make revisions where necessary to:
  - Overall process
  - Questions
  - Moderator approach
  - Room arrangement

---

## VII. Sampling & Recruitment
- Establish criteria for selecting / sampling focus group participants:
  - Group size
  - Demographics
  - Familiarity with researcher
- Develop specifications for participation
- Develop nomination list
- Prepare invitation list

---

## VIII. Equipment & Materials
- Acquire / construct appropriate data collection & recording instruments:
  - Video camera
  - Film
  - Computer
  - Cassette recorder
  - Observation journal
- Ensure all equipment is working properly

---

## IX. Focus Group Session

### Session Setup
- Compose focus group agenda
- Prepare room for session:
  - Media equipment
  - Paper
  - Pens
  - Recorders
  - Refreshments

### Session Conduct
- Obtain background information on participants
- Arrange questions from general to specific
- Carefully & thoroughly monitor and record:
  - Observations
  - Dialogue
  - Reactions
  - Facial expressions
  - Shifts in intonation
- Clarify ambiguous comments made by participants
- Draw diagram of seating chart for later reference
- Probe where necessary to obtain clarity or inconsistent comments
- Provide summary comments for group to ensure researcher’s notes reflect participants’ intent
- Monitor recording equipment to ensure proper functioning

---

## X. Immediate Debriefing
- Debrief immediately following focus group with:
  - Assistants
  - Moderator
- Document notable:
  - Observations
  - Comments
  - Impressions
- Place necessary labels on notes, tapes, and materials

---

## XI. Point of Data Reduction
Researchers collect data according to what they consider “worthy” of research, which is shaped by:
- Historical conditions
- Race
- Class
- Gender
- Ethnicity

> It is important to record **all** data as it is seen and heard, and later filter what is not necessary.

---

## XII. Data Organization & Analysis
- Refine concepts & revise:
  - Relationship models
  - Assumptions
  - Hypotheses
  - Research approach
- Organize & refine:
  - Tapes
  - Transcripts
  - Field notes
  - Observations
- Make back-ups of tapes / cassettes
- Enter data into computer
- Fill in missing information
- Look for:
  - Emerging themes
  - Developing categories
- Diagram relationships and themes
- Interpret & analyze data
- Summarize findings

---

## XIII. Interpretation & Implications
- Determine meaning & implications of findings in relation to research concern / issue
- Propose practical recommendations for:
  - Addressing concern / issue
  - Future research

---

## XIV. Reporting & Application
- Report & present findings in:
  - Documents
  - Publications
  - Presentations
- Assess:
  - Theoretical implications
  - Practical implications
  - Ethical implications
  - Methodological implications
- Develop policy, standards, or guiding principles according to lessons learned

# Survey Research  
## Quantitative Data Collection & Analysis Framework

---

## I. Objective Conditions
- Researcher’s:
  - Race
  - Class
  - Gender
  - Age
  - Ethnicity
  - Nationality

> These objective conditions influence what is considered worthy of research and how data is interpreted.

---

## II. Purpose Test
**Is the purpose of the research to gather information from a population and draw inductive inferences from data?**

- **NO** → Select another research design  
- **YES** → Continue

---

## III. Identify the Research Problem
- Identify the research problem
- Conduct literature & preliminary review to clearly define the research problem
- Draft a precise statement of the problem and context in which it emerged

---

## IV. Plan of Action
Construct a plan of action considering:
- Description of population
- Sampling design
- Data collection design
- Data analysis design
- Information & resource needs
- Available resources
- Timeframe

- Develop a draft project flow chart for reference

---

## V. Comprehensive Literature Review
- Conduct a comprehensive literature review
- Critically analyze:
  - Biases
  - Findings
  - Procedures
- Establish chronological, historical, and logical order
- Clarify independent & dependent variables and concepts

---

## VI. Theoretical Framework & Hypothesis
- Develop a measurable research hypothesis / research question
- Define independent & dependent variables
- Establish relationships among variables
- Develop valid & reliable measures for dependent variables

---

## VII. Population & Sampling
- Identify population
- Decide:
  - **Sample**
  - **Census**
- Determine sample size
- Select sampling design:

### Random Sampling
- Simple random
- Systematic
- Multistage random
- Stratified random
- Cluster
- Stratified cluster

### Non-Random Sampling
- Stratified disproportionate
- Judgment
- Quota
- Snowball

---

## VIII. Study Time Frame
- Establish study time frame:
  - **Cross-sectional**: people surveyed at one point in time
  - **Longitudinal**: same group surveyed two or more times (e.g., panel or cohort)

---

## IX. Statistical & Analysis Design
Select appropriate test statistics & statistical analysis design considering:
- Level of measurement:
  - Nominal
  - Ordinal
  - Interval
  - Ratio
- Number of variables:
  - Dependent (single / multiple)
  - Independent (single / multiple)

### Descriptive Statistics
- Mode
- Median
- Mean
- Range
- Standard deviation
- Variance

### Measures of Association / Correlation
- Chi-square
- Pearson r
- Spearman rho
- Kendall’s tau
- Point-biserial correlation

### Group Comparison
- t-test
- Chi-square goodness of fit
- Analysis of variance (ANOVA)
- Fisher’s exact test

---

## X. Data Collection Design
- Determine appropriate response format:
  - Nominal
  - Ordinal
  - Interval
  - Ratio
- Select data collection method:
  - Interview
  - Phone
  - Self-administered (mail, email, online, etc.)

---

## XI. Instrument Development
- Use existing instrument?
  - **YES** → Adopt & adapt
  - **NO** → Develop new instrument
- Construct questionnaire appropriate to data collection design
- Check wording of each question:
  - Avoid ambiguity
  - Avoid bias
  - Avoid offensive or leading language
- Construct codebook & response coding scheme

---

## XII. Pilot Study
- Conduct pilot / pre-test:
  - Draw sample
  - Conduct pilot
  - Evaluate instrument
- Revise instrument, codebook, process, and/or procedures as needed

---

## XIII. Administration
- Train staff according to data collection design
- Administer survey via:
  - Mail
  - Interview
  - Telephone
  - Email
  - Online platforms
  - Other means

---

## XIV. Data Processing
- Gather, organize, and process raw data
- Clean errors & bias
- Determine if data should be condensed & simplified:
  - Prepare frequency distribution by grouping arrayed data into classes
- Prepare graphic display if needed:
  - Histogram
  - Polygon
  - Ogive

---

## XV. Data Analysis
- Compute measures:
  - Frequency
  - Proportion
  - Percent
  - Central tendency
  - Variability
  - Association
  - Linear relationship
- Establish purpose of statistical analysis
- Summarize univariate data
- Explore relationships between variables
- Assess boundaries & influential forces in the system
- Analyze data using appropriate statistical techniques

---

## XVI. Interpretation & Conclusions
- Draw conclusions
- Prepare research report
- Publish report / present findings
- Apply findings to practice, policy, or further research
