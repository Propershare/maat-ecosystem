**Comprehensive Evidence Lifecycle Table (Physical Science, Social Science, Crime, Law)**

| Step | Stage of Evidence Handling | What Counts as Evidence | How It Should Be Done (Scientific & Legal Standards) | Purpose / Outcome |
|------|----------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | Define the Research or Legal Question | Evidence is anything capable of answering a falsifiable question (measurement, observation, record, testimony, artifact). | Clearly state the hypothesis, research question, legal claim, or charge. Define variables, time period, population, and scope. | Prevents irrelevant, biased, or unfocused evidence collection. |
| 2 | Identify Evidence Types | Physical (objects, measurements), Documentary (records, texts), Testimonial (interviews, witness statements), Digital (logs, metadata), Statistical (datasets). | Match evidence types to the question. In law, distinguish direct vs circumstantial evidence; in science, primary vs secondary data. | Ensures the right form of evidence is gathered. |
| 3 | Establish Standards & Protocols | Evidence must meet methodological, ethical, and legal admissibility standards. | Predefine protocols: sampling methods, chain-of-custody rules, consent, instrumentation standards, data formats. | Guarantees consistency, reproducibility, and admissibility. |
| 4 | Collect Evidence | Raw observations, measurements, recordings, samples, interviews, documents. | Use controlled instruments, standardized surveys, calibrated tools, forensic procedures, or authenticated data sources. Log time, location, collector. | Preserves originality and traceability of evidence. |
| 5 | Document Metadata | Contextual information about the evidence. | Record who collected it, when, where, how, conditions, instruments used, and any limitations. | Allows verification and replication. |
| 6 | Preserve Original Evidence | Untouched primary data or material. | Secure physical evidence; write-protect digital data; store originals separately from working copies. | Prevents contamination, tampering, or loss. |
| 7 | Initial Screening | Potentially flawed or irrelevant evidence. | Remove duplicates, corrupted files, unusable samples, or irrelevant materials without altering originals. | Improves efficiency while preserving integrity. |
| 8 | Data Cleaning | Errors, noise, inconsistencies. | Correct measurement errors, transcription mistakes; standardize units; flag missing data; document all changes. | Produces analyzable data without falsification. |
| 9 | Bias Detection | Systematic distortions. | Check for sampling bias, observer bias, instrument bias, confirmation bias, or coercion (especially in testimony). | Prevents distorted conclusions. |
| 10 | Verification | Authenticity and accuracy of evidence. | Cross-check sources, replicate measurements, confirm document authenticity, corroborate testimony. | Confirms evidence is real and accurate. |
| 11 | Validation | Relevance and reliability. | Ensure evidence actually measures what it claims (construct validity), is consistent (reliability), and applies to the question. | Confirms evidentiary value. |
| 12 | Chain of Custody (Crime/Law) | Legal integrity of evidence. | Maintain a continuous documented record of possession, transfer, and storage. | Protects admissibility in court. |
| 13 | Analytical Processing | Interpreted evidence. | Apply statistical analysis, qualitative coding, forensic testing, or modeling using transparent methods. | Converts raw evidence into findings. |
| 14 | Error & Uncertainty Assessment | Limitations of evidence. | Calculate margins of error, confidence intervals, reliability coefficients; disclose uncertainty. | Prevents overclaiming. |
| 15 | Triangulation | Multiple evidence streams. | Compare independent data sources, methods, or perspectives to confirm consistency. | Strengthens conclusions. |
| 16 | Classification & Organization | Structured evidence system. | Categorize by type, source, time, relevance, and reliability; use standardized naming and indexing. | Enables retrieval and review. |
| 17 | Secure Storage | Long-term preservation. | Physical: climate-controlled, locked storage. Digital: encrypted databases, backups, access logs. | Prevents loss and unauthorized access. |
| 18 | Ethical & Legal Compliance | Human and legal protections. | Anonymize data, protect privacy, comply with consent, evidence rules, and data protection laws. | Prevents harm and legal violations. |
| 19 | Presentation Preparation | Communicable evidence. | Select representative data; create tables, charts, exhibits, transcripts, or summaries without distortion. | Makes evidence understandable. |
| 20 | Transparent Presentation | Public, scientific, or legal disclosure. | Present methods, data sources, limitations, and findings clearly; separate facts from interpretation. | Ensures scrutiny and trust. |
| 21 | Peer Review / Adversarial Testing | Independent evaluation. | Subject evidence to peer review, replication, cross-examination, or legal challenge. | Tests robustness. |
