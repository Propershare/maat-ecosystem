**Mathematical Foundations of Kemet (Ancient Kmt) and Their Later Misattribution to Greece**

| No. | Mathematical Concept / Field | Kmtian Source & Transliteration | Translation / Definition | Mathematical Formulas & Principles Known in Kemet | Example / Application in Kemet | Date (BCE) | Later Greek Attribution | Time Gap (Years) | Comparative Evidence / Notes |
|-----|------------------------------|----------------------------------|--------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|-------------|----------------------------|------------------|------------------------------|
| 1 | Right-Angle Geometry (3–4–5 Triangle) | 3ꜥ r-md.t (Moscow Papyrus, Kahun Papyrus) | “Mathematical Document”; “Surveyor’s Manual” | Relationship of sides in right triangle: 3² + 4² = 5² | Used for land-surveying, temple foundations, and pyramid base-squaring | 4300–1800 | Attributed to Pythagoras | 3300–2500 | Berlin Papyrus shows same triangle centuries before Pythagoras; Kmtians used it practically; Greeks formalized proof. |
| 2 | “The Pythagorean Theorem” | 3ꜥ r-md.t (Berlin Papyrus Problem 6619) | “To find the side of a rectangle divided into two right triangles” | a² + b² = c² | Used for layout of fields and pyramid bases | ~3800 | Pythagoras (c. 500) | ~3300 | Kmtians solved geometrically, not symbolically—proofs unnecessary in practical state architecture. |
| 3 | Geometry of Solids (Frustum Volume) | 3ꜥ r-md.t (Moscow Papyrus Problem 10) | “Method of calculating a truncated pyramid’s volume” | V = (1/3) x (a² + a*b + b²) | Formula for frustum volume (used in step-pyramid cores and mastabas) | ~3850 | Euclid, *Elements*, Book XII | ~2550 | Oldest known example of solid geometry—preceded Greek mathematics by over a millennium. |
| 4 | Area of Circle & Pi (π) | 3ꜥ r-md.t (Rhind Papyrus Problem 50) | “Method of finding the area of a circle” | A = (8/9 Ø)² → π ≈ 3.1605 | Used in circular silos and architectural bases | ~3650 | Archimedes (287–212) | ~2400 | Kmtian π more accurate than early Greek approximations; 1st recorded rational approximation of π. |
| 5 | Trigonometry / Slope Calculation (Seked) | šdꜥ – *Seked* | “Slope” or “Inclination” | Seked = (horizontal run in palms) / (1 cubit rise) | Great Pyramid’s *seked* = 5.5 palms ≈ 51.84° | ~3600 | Hipparchus, *Table of Chords* (150) | ~2450 | *Seked* = cotangent(θ); Kmtians used functional trigonometry ~2,000 years earlier. |
| 6 | Algebra (Linear & Quadratic Equations) | dꜥ.n – “to compute”; rꜥ–nꜥ – “fractions” / “parts” | Practical algebraic calculation | Example: x + ½x + 1/7x = 19; solve for x → x = 16 | Used in grain division, taxation, and logistics | 3650–1650 | Diophantus, *Arithmetica* (250) | ~3000 | Kmtians had symbolic algebraic thinking without letters; solved by arithmetic substitution. |
| 7 | Fractions & Number Theory | rꜥ–nꜥ – *Part* / Fraction | Unit fractions (1/n) | All fractions expressed as sums of unit fractions; e.g. 2/5 = 1/3 + 1/15 | Grain, rations, tax computation | ~4000 | Greeks (later: rational fractions) | ~1000 | Rhind Papyrus’ 2/n table shows complete rational decomposition — pure number theory. |
| 8 | Euclidean Algorithm (GCD; Division) | rꜥ–nꜥ methods in Rhind Papyrus | “Portioning” / “division” | Iterative subtraction to find greatest common divisor | Used to divide bread, beer, grain | ~2650 | Euclid, *Elements*, Book VII | ~2350 | Algorithm identical to Euclid’s; Kemet used it for practical proportional sharing. |
| 9 | Astronomical Geometry (Cardinal Alignment) | pt – “Sky”; mḫt “Merḫet” (plumb-line tool) | “Observation of the sky” | Align pyramid sides by simultaneous transit of circumpolar stars (Mizar & Kochab) | Pyramid: true north within ±0.05° | ~4300 | Hipparchus (Celestial geometry, 150) | ~2450 | Stellar alignment predates Greek astronomy; precise to modern technology. |
| 10 | Solar & Stellar Timekeeping | spdt (Sirius); ꜥḫ (Orion) | “Divine star of Isis” / “Osirian constellation” | Observation of Sirius calendar—365.25 days | Used for calendar and agricultural timing | ~4300 | Eudoxus & Hipparchus | ~2600 | Kmtian Sothic cycle (1461 years) shows deep-rooted astronomical periodic understanding. |
| 11 | Golden Ratio (Φ ≈ 1.618) | šdꜥ (slope) – implicit in pyramid design | “Inclination measure” | Great Pyramid’s angle encodes golden triangle | Used for aesthetic proportions | ~4300 | Pythagoreans & Euclid (*Elements*) | ~3000 | Pyramid’s *seked* used for aesthetic proportional design; Greeks drew from Egyptian design. |
| 12 | Pi and Earth Geometry-Encoding | bnbn.t – “Pyramid” | Sacred architectural planning | Perimeter / Height ≈ 2πr; Latitude 30°N encodes Earth’s geometry | Giza pyramid encodes π, φ, and Earth radius | ~4300 | No Greek equivalent until Eratosthenes (240 BCE) | ~4000 | Pyramid’s ratio shows geodesic understanding—Earth radius ~43,200 = height ratio. |
