### Corrections and Corrective Systems for Black Liberation Organization

#### Former Weaknesses Identified (1960s–1970s) | Corrective Scientific Principle | Key Actions & Methods for the New System | Expected Outcome
---|---|---|---
Movements centered on charismatic leaders; lacked institutional structure and continuity. | Build **cellular, tiered, and institutional organization** guided by collective leadership. | - Form disciplined planning cores with collective decision-making.  
- Establish departments for education, economy, defense, and culture.  
- Build autonomous but coordinated local units.  
- Emphasize trained competence and moral integrity over personality. | Stable, leader-independent organization with sustained moral and technical excellence.
Emotional reactions replaced strategic planning; movements lacked comprehensive social and economic blueprints. | Operate on a **planned economy of struggle** — integrate short-, medium-, and long-term development goals. | - Design multi-decade programs for education, economy, defense, and consciousness.  
- Build systems for policy research and data analysis.  
- Implement phased strategic goals with measurable indicators.  
- Connect activism to material institution-building. | Rational, adaptive development focused on core social advancement.
Leadership rose without ideological testing or moral vetting; led to infiltration and instability. | Establish **strict initiation and Seba selection** based on ideological, moral, and psychological discipline. | - Screen members through stages of ideological study, service, and self-assessment.  
- Test discipline, humility, and loyalty through community projects.  
- Conduct peer and community review before promotion.  
- Reject ego-based or opportunistic leadership. | Core of loyal, principled cadres with long-term commitment.
Informal and inconsistent leadership education; lack of structured mentorship. | Create **systematic leadership schools** with continuous theoretical and practical education. | - Establish institutions for political, cultural, and technical education.  
- Integrate classroom study with fieldwork and administration.  
- Base promotion on study, service, and demonstrated leadership.  
- Rotate members across roles for comprehensive training. | Continuously replenished leadership core.
No distinction between Sebas and general members; led to confusion, infiltration, and emotional instability. | Build **dual structure** — disciplined core Sebas guiding organized mass participation. | - Create tiers for general membership and core leadership.  
- Educate masses through cooperative work, culture, and practical instruction.  
- Separate moral hierarchy of responsibility. | Harmonized unity of leadership and grassroots.
Erosion of moral discipline; emotional fragility; led to instability, drug abuse, and disillusionment. | Integrate **moral, cultural, and family ethics** into the revolutionary system. | - Center value systems in education and organization.  
- Create written constitutions and family codes.  
- Build village models of social responsibility and justice. | Scientific fusion of moral and social advancement.
Lack of institutional procedures for training, correction, or internal evaluation; led to dead ends and factionalism. | Create **corrective systems and performance evaluation** at all levels. | - Develop step-by-step guides for evaluation and conflict resolution.  
- Establish clear discipline procedures, appeals systems, and project reviews. | A self-sustaining culture of correction and improvement.
Movements pursued revolution without infrastructure; burned out. | Practice **strategic patience** — revolution as process, not event. | - Develop stepwise long-term projects in education, science, economy, and political structure.  
- Build revolutionary time consciousness across generations. | Generation-spanning revolutionary continuity.
Fragmented emotional slogans substituted for actual theory or institutions. | Replace **charisma with structure, emotion with science, and slogans with systems**. | - Build integrated systems for policy (education, defense, economy).  
- Replace informal rhetoric with technical excellence.  
- Foster generational continuity. | An enduring, self-replicating civilizational project.
