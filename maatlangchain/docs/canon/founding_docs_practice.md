**STEP-BY-STEP SCIENTIFIC PRACTICE FOR DEVELOPING FOUNDING DOCUMENTS, POLICIES & PROCEDURES**

1. **Founding documents must be precise, not improvised.**  
Doctrines and policies are the blueprint for how the organization will run for decades. They need consistency, internal logic, and tested language. A conference with random or planted participants produces improvisation, opinion, and noise instead of a disciplined framework.

2. **Random voices lack the necessary expertise.**  
Creating doctrine requires deep knowledge: legal, historical, organizational science, systems design, and risk management. People "off the street" don’t bring this specialized expertise. They can contribute lived experience later in consultation, but not at the foundation-writing stage.

3. **Risk of manipulation by "planted" participants.**  
If the room includes planted actors, the discussion can be captured by hidden interests, steering the outcome toward political, financial, or personal agendas. This weakens the scientific neutrality and integrity of the documents.

4. **Noise drowns out signal.**  
Large, uncontrolled conferences generate speculation, opinions, and debates rather than hard evidence. This muddies the process and delays actual work. Strong organizations filter input through structured consultation after a draft framework already exists.

5. **Lack of accountability.**  
Founding documents must be owned by clearly identified drafters and approvers who can be held accountable for errors or omissions. A "conference of strangers" disperses responsibility — no one can be held to account for mistakes.

6. **Breakdown in organizational discipline.**  
Organizations succeed when they function like systems — with inputs, processes, and outputs. Letting doctrine be drafted in a free-for-all setting undermines discipline, leaving future staff unsure whether to follow official procedures or "what someone said at the conference."

7. **Best practice: evidence-led drafting, controlled consultation.**  
The scientific method in organization-building is:
- → Draft by experts with system design training.
- → Test with pilots and simulations.
- → Vet with controlled expert panels.
- → Consult affected groups in a structured, focused way (not random open conferences). This way, lived experience and public insight refine the system after a scientific backbone exists.
