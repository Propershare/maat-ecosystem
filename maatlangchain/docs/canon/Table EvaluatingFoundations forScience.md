# Table: Evaluating Foundations for Science

| **Type** | **Definition/Use** | **Why It Fails as a Foundation for Science** | **Proper Use** |
|----------|--------------------|---------------------------------------------|----------------|
| **Anecdote** | Telling events or data in a narrative form. | Selective, interpretive, and prone to distortion; not objective evidence. | Can illustrate findings; accessible, but not foundational. |
| **Speculation** | Imagining explanations or scenarios. | Produces speculation without verification; indistinguishable from fantasy. | Useful for hypotheses, but must be tested against evidence. |
| **Name-Calling** | Using labels or insults in place of reasoning. | Substitutes social pressure for logic; undermines objectivity. | No legitimate role; should be avoided entirely. |
| **Analogy** | Comparing new or complex ideas to familiar ones. | Similarity does not prove equivalence; risks false assumptions. | Helpful for explanation; never proof. |
| **Opinion** | Personal beliefs, judgments, or preferences. | Subjective and shaped by bias; do not change facts. | Can inspire inquiry; must be challenged and revised through evidence. |
| **Diversion** | Shifting attention away from the issue or flooding with irrelevance. | Obscures direct engagement with data; confuses inquiry. | None—science must stay focused. |
| **Assumption** | Believing what might be true without proof. | Produces unfounded claims; creates “castles in the air.” | Can inspire creative thought; not a scientific foundation. |
| **Assertion** | Claiming or reasoning without supporting evidence. | Relies on persuasion or assertion, not proof; cannot be verified. | Must be evidence-based to be valid. |
| **Appeal to Authority** | Assuming something is true because an expert or admired figure said so. | Authority does not equal evidence; even experts can be wrong. | Expert input can guide inquiry, but claims must be tested. |
| **Slander** | Making damaging or false claims about people instead of addressing ideas. | Attacks individuals rather than evaluating data; destroys trust in inquiry. | No role—science requires civility and focus on data. |
| **Evidence** | Observable, measurable data from experiments, observation, or documentation. | Provides verifiable, reproducible grounding for conclusions. | The foundation of reliable knowledge. |
