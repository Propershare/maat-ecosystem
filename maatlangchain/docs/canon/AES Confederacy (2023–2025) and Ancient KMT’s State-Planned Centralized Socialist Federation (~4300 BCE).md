## Key Principles of Central Authority

- The more states choose by design, prioritizing local autonomy, the more fragile the federation.
- The more states fully federate, the more coherent the federal entities become.
- Confederations retain competing central elites, tribes, or states—diluting authority.
- Federations act as one command capable of directing resources, making binding laws, or enforcing collective security.
- Historical analog: Greek city-states or the Delian League never consolidated into a federation, leaving them fractured and vulnerable to Macedon and later Rome.

## Economic Coordination

- AES has yet to integrate economies (currency, taxation, infrastructure), which slows growth.
- Kmt planned food, irrigation, labor across provinces, building shared prosperity.

## Unification Dynamics

- Confederacies negotiate, extend unified rivals act decisively.
- Federations project power militarily, and politically while the confederation stagnates.
- Historical lesson: If states do not voluntarily evolve into a federation, unification often comes later through war, conquest, or external crisis, not peaceful institutional growth.
