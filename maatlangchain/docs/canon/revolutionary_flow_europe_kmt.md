**A) Europe’s Revolutionary Flow of Forms (Stepwise)**

| Step | Form (Europe) | Core Relations of Production | Key Contradictions | Regression Before the Leap | Outcome/Next Form |
|------|----------------|-------------------------------|--------------------|----------------------------|--------------------|
| 1 | Primitive-communalism/hunter-gatherer | Kin-based subsistence, shared means | Low surplus vs. growing needs | — | Emergence of class, state, slavery |
| 2 | Ancient-slavery (Greece/Rome) | Master-slave, expansion by conquest | Limits of extensive growth; revolts; costs of empire | Attempts to intensify extraction | Disintegration → localized agrarian orders |
| 3 | Feudalism | Lord-serf, land-rent, Church hegemony | Peasant resistance; town/market expansion | Feudal lords revive slave-like forms in peripheries | Proto-bourgeois towns, markets, credit expansion |
| 4 | Mercantilism & colonial plunder | Merchant capital, protected trade | Need for cheap labor & raw inputs | Transatlantic slavery modeled on Rome | Primitive accumulation for industry |
| 5 | Industrial capitalism | Wage labor, factory systems | Overproduction vs. limited wages/markets | Coercive labor regimes in colonies | Global expansion of capital relations |
| 6 | Mature/global capitalism | Integration of mining, agriculture, construction, services | Falling wages, crises, imperial rivalry | Financialization, exploitation in periphery | Monopoly/finance capital dominance |
| 7 | Automation/robotics phase | Capital substitutes labor at scale | Shrinking consumer base; profit-demand gap | Precarity, platformization, surveillance labor | Structural crisis deepens |
| 8 | Transition (open) | — | Intensified systemic contradictions | Reversion to older forms (gig/indenture-like) | Contest over post-capitalist ordering |
