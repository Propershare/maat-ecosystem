## What it would look like in modern times: African Federal Governance

The modernized Kmtian federal system is organized as a centralized, state-planned government whose purpose is to harmonize political authority, economic development, scientific advancement, social welfare, cultural preservation, and ethical conduct within a single integrated national framework. The entire system is guided by the principle of Maat, understood not merely as a religious concept but as a practical standard of social balance, justice, truth, order, responsibility, and collective well-being. Every institution, from the highest national council to the smallest local administration, functions according to a unified national plan designed to advance the prosperity and stability of society as a whole.

**National Federal Society (apex).** At the apex of the system stands the National Federal Society, the supreme governing authority responsible for determining the long-term direction of the nation. This body unites political leadership, economic planning, scientific research, cultural administration, ethical guidance, and national development under a single strategic vision. Rather than allowing competing institutions to pursue separate agendas, the supreme authority coordinates all major sectors of society according to comprehensive development plans extending decades into the future. Through continuous study of social conditions, economic performance, technological progress, demographic trends, environmental realities, and cultural needs, the national leadership establishes goals that guide every level of government.

**Federal Planning Council (central nervous system).** Directly beneath the supreme authority operates the Federal Planning Council, which serves as the central nervous system of the entire economy. This institution gathers statistical information from every region, industry, school, hospital, transportation network, and research center in the nation. Using this information, planners establish production targets, infrastructure priorities, scientific objectives, educational requirements, and social welfare goals. Long-term plans define the broad developmental trajectory of the nation, while five-year and annual plans translate those objectives into measurable programs. Resources, labor, technology, and capital are allocated according to national priorities rather than the pursuit of private gain. The purpose of planning is to ensure that production serves social needs, eliminates waste, balances regional development, and continuously improves the material conditions of the population.

**Executive ministries (implementation).** The executive branch is organized into specialized ministries responsible for implementing national plans. Ministries oversee governance, economic development, agriculture, industry, transportation, architecture, education, healthcare, science, technology, culture, media, ethics, and defense. Each ministry develops detailed operational programs based on objectives established by the national plan. Agricultural ministries coordinate food production and irrigation systems. Industrial ministries direct manufacturing, energy generation, and resource extraction. Transportation ministries oversee railways, roads, ports, aviation, and urban transit. Housing and architectural ministries guide construction and urban development according to national standards. Every ministry functions as a component of a larger integrated system whose purpose is to advance collective prosperity and social stability.

**Provinces (nomes / sepats).** The nation is divided into provinces modeled after the historical nomes, sepats, or states of ancient Kmt. Provincial governments serve as administrative extensions of the national government and are responsible for implementing federal plans within their territories. Provincial councils coordinate economic activity, infrastructure development, scientific research, educational administration, healthcare services, and cultural programs. Although provinces possess administrative flexibility, they operate within the framework established by national planning authorities. Their role is not to pursue independent development strategies but to adapt national objectives to local conditions while maintaining overall unity.

**Municipal governments (foundation).** Municipal governments form the foundation of everyday administration. Cities, towns, and local communities are responsible for operating schools, hospitals, transportation systems, public housing, utilities, recreational facilities, and local services. Municipal plans are developed in coordination with provincial and federal objectives to ensure that local development contributes to broader national goals. Through this structure, every community becomes an active participant in the implementation of national development strategies.

**Legal system (Maat as law).** The legal system functions as an instrument for maintaining social harmony and public accountability. Law is understood as a practical expression of Maat and is intended to preserve balance, fairness, transparency, and justice. Courts operate at municipal, provincial, and national levels, while legal councils ensure consistency throughout the federation. Criminal justice emphasizes restoration and rehabilitation whenever possible, seeking to reintegrate individuals into society rather than relying exclusively on punitive measures. Police, prosecutors, courts, and correctional institutions function as interconnected components of a system designed to protect public order while promoting ethical conduct and social responsibility.

**Education (foundation of national progress).** Education occupies a central position within the state because the development of human knowledge is viewed as the foundation of national progress. Universal education begins in childhood and extends through advanced scientific and professional training. Schools combine literacy, mathematics, science, history, technical skills, and ethical education into a unified curriculum. Vocational institutions prepare skilled workers for agriculture, industry, transportation, construction, healthcare, and technology. Universities and research academies train scientists, engineers, physicians, educators, and administrators. Educational planning is coordinated directly with economic planning so that the number of trained professionals corresponds to national developmental needs.

**Science and technology (strategic instruments).** Science and technology are treated as strategic instruments of civilization-building. A nationwide network of research institutes, laboratories, universities, and innovation centers conducts both fundamental and applied research. Scientific inquiry is directed toward solving practical problems, increasing productivity, improving healthcare, expanding knowledge, protecting the environment, and advancing technological capabilities. Research priorities are aligned with national objectives, ensuring that scientific resources contribute directly to social development and material improvement.

**Healthcare (universal public system).** Healthcare is organized as a universal public system accessible to all citizens. Community clinics provide primary care, district hospitals deliver specialized treatment, and national medical centers conduct advanced research and training. Public health campaigns focus on prevention, sanitation, nutrition, physical fitness, and disease control. Healthcare is regarded as a social responsibility rather than a commodity, and medical planning is integrated into broader national development strategies.

**Mass media (public education).** Mass media functions as an institution of public education, civic formation, and cultural development. News agencies, broadcasting systems, publishing organizations, and digital communications networks are organized to disseminate accurate information, promote scientific literacy, preserve cultural heritage, and strengthen social cohesion. Rather than operating primarily as commercial enterprises, media institutions serve educational and developmental purposes within society.

**Military (defense and development).** The military serves as both a defense organization and a national development institution. Armed forces protect territorial integrity and national sovereignty, while engineering units contribute to infrastructure construction and disaster response. Military education emphasizes discipline, technical competence, ethical conduct, and public service. National martial arts programs encourage physical fitness, self-discipline, and community responsibility among citizens.

**Culture (living national identity).** Culture occupies a vital place within the system. Museums, archives, libraries, artistic institutions, and cultural academies preserve historical memory while encouraging creative expression. Traditional knowledge, language, architecture, literature, music, and philosophy are maintained as living components of national identity. Cultural development is viewed not as a luxury but as an essential element of social cohesion and collective self-understanding.

**Transportation (unified public network).** Transportation is organized as a unified public network connecting all regions of the federation. Railways, highways, waterways, ports, airports, and urban transit systems are planned and managed as integrated components of national development. Efficient transportation facilitates economic coordination, social mobility, scientific exchange, and cultural interaction while ensuring balanced regional growth.

**Maat (ethical bedrock).** Underlying every institution is the ethical principle of Maat. Ethical education begins in childhood and continues throughout life through schools, workplaces, media, public institutions, and civic organizations. Citizens are encouraged to cultivate honesty, responsibility, discipline, service, respect for knowledge, and commitment to the common good. Ethics is therefore not confined to personal behavior but becomes a guiding principle for governance, economics, science, education, and public life.

**Operational logic (research → plan → implement → evaluate → correct).** The operational logic of the entire system follows a continuous cycle of research, planning, implementation, evaluation, and correction. Scientific institutions collect information. Planning agencies analyze data and establish objectives. Government ministries implement programs. Audit and inspection bodies measure outcomes. Results are reviewed by national leadership and incorporated into future plans. Through this process, the state functions as a continuously learning and self-correcting system dedicated to increasing knowledge, improving living standards, advancing technology, strengthening social harmony, and ensuring the long-term development of civilization.
