# Historical Evolution of the Enslavement of Africans: From Arab to European Systems

| Date Range | Key Actors / Invaders | Main Events & Processes | Centers, Routes & Mechanisms of Enslavement | Cultural, Political, and Social Consequences |
|------------|------------------------|--------------------------|---------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Before 656 BCE | Indigenous Africans (Kmt / Nile Valley Civilizations) | African Kmt (Egypt) functioned as a complex, moral, and scientific society without chattel slavery. Labor was organized through communal, state, and temple service—not ownership of humans. | State labor for irrigation, agriculture, pyramid-building—within kinship-based citizenship system. | Balanced social-organization model; foundation for later Arab domination. |
| 656 BCE–642 CE | Assyrians → Persians → Greeks → Romans → Vandals | Continuous waves of foreign conquest eroded African sovereignty in Kmt and North Africa. | Armies from Western Asia entered via the Sinai and Nile Delta. | African institutions weakened, setting the stage for later Arab domination. |
| 642 CE onward | Arab Caliphate under Amr ibn al-'As | Arabs seized Egypt from Byzantine rule; renamed the capital Cairo (al-Qahirah); integrated North Africa into the Arab-Islamic world. | Desert and sea invasions: from Arabia through the Red Sea, Sinai, and Maghreb. | Collapse of African Kmt; rise of Islamic administration; beginning of Arabization. |
| 7th–19th centuries | Umayyad, Abbasid, and later Sultanates (Oman, Zanzibar, Yemen) | Arabs developed a systematic enslavement economy combining religion, trade, and imperial expansion. Africans captured inland and sold through desert and coastal routes. | Slave centers: Cairo, Muscat, Jiddah, Aden, Mecca, Zeila, Zanzibar, Kilwa, Malindi, Mogadishu → Markets: Mecca, Zabid, Muscat, Aden, Socotra, Massawa, Zeila, Bagamoyo, Sofala. | Arabic word **"abd" (means slave/Black); codified racial slavery in Islamicate law and culture; widespread castration and assimilation. |
| 711 CE— | Omani Sultanate & Red Sea traders | Arabs captured Zanzibar and much of the East African coast (7th C); built fortresses and port towns for export of Africans. | Bagamoyo routes → East Africa → Arabia → Persia → India → China. | The Indian Ocean Slave Trade exported Africans from East Africa for centuries. |
| 1500–1888 CE | Portuguese Empire, Spanish Empire | Portuguese joined Arab traders along East African coast; Spain and Portugal led slave raids in West Africa and exported millions to the Americas. | Kilwa, Mombasa, and Zanzibar ports controlled. | Christianity introduced; slave economy begins feeding into global capitalism. |
| 1500s–1888 CE | Dutch → Spanish → British → French → Germans | European powers established transatlantic slave systems: plantation slavery, factories, and military posts. | Routes: West Africa → Caribbean → North + South America. | Genocidal depopulation of West Africa; creation of Afro-Diaspora. |
| ca. 650 CE–1900 CE | Arab and European slaveholders | Arab and European enslavement lasted over 1200 years. | Across Sahara, Sahel, Red Sea, and Atlantic systems overlap. | Structural underdevelopment of Africa and demographic devastation. |
| ca. 1800s–1900s | Ottoman Empire, Arab caliphates | Language as weapon: **"Abd" → "Black" → enslaved. | Arabic codified racial identity and religious justification for enslavement. | Ideological legacy of Arab enslavement outlasted legal abolition. |
