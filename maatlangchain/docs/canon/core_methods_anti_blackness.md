## CORE METHOD(S) (HOW ANTI-BLACKNESS OPERATED)

| Era / Sphere | Core method(s) (how anti-Blackness operated) | Institutional instruments (places-practices lived) | Dominant ideological justification(s) | Representative evidence / examples |
|--------------|-----------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Ancient (Greece, Rome, Nile Valley) | Cultural appropriation + social exclusion: claim African knowledge, deny African agency; mark Africans as natural slaves/others | Conquest administration, slavery, elite literature, theater, temples, school curricula. "A murderous siege of invasion and parasitic predation." | Civilizational hierarchy (Greco-Roman ideal); "barbarian" discourse; pagan philosophical hierarchies | Alexander's appropriation of Kmtian priestly knowledge; Roman slavery and literary depictions that exoticize and denigrate Africans; archaeological traces of African captives in Mediterranean contexts |
| Early Medieval—Arab-Islamic expansions | Militarized conversion + enslavement; juridical codification of slaves' status; gendered-sexual exploitation; cultural erasure | Caliphal/state-law, religious jurisprudence (fiqh), trans-Saharan caravan networks, courts, harem/slave economies. Mass murderous enslavement, rapes, making of eunuchs, and sex slaves | Religious/ritual hierarchies recoded into racialized servitude (often with theological rationales about status) | Chronicled Arab conquests of North Africa; long-term trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean slave trades; records of slave markets and juridical rulings regulating slaves' status |
| European colonialism & Atlantic slave system | Systemic commodification (mass-forced migration); labor extraction at industrial-scale; legal-racialization | Colonial-law, mercantile trade networks, slave codes, plantation regimes, scientific institutions, missionary schools. Rape, breeding, lynching, whippings, hangings | "Scientific racism," biblical or natural-order justifications, doctrine of civilizing missions | Transatlantic slave trade statistics; plantation economies in the Americas; slave codes (e.g., Virginia), Enlightenment period racial taxonomy and later eugenics |
| Early U.S. state formation | Constitutional and legal embedding of racial hierarchy; settler-colonial land-clearing + chattel slavery as state foundations | Constitutions/statutes, state militias, land courts, political representation rules | Constitutional compromise that treats Blacks as less-than-full persons; racial capitalism logic | Three-Fifths Compromise, slave codes; dispossession of Indigenous lands integrated with plantation development |
| Post-Emancipation → Jim Crow | Reconstitution of social control through segregation, disenfranchisement, extra-legal terror (lynching) | Local and state-law (Black Codes), police, courts, vigilante violence, educational segregation | White supremacy as social order; pseudo-legal color-line; “separate but equal” doctrine | Black Codes; Plessy v. Ferguson; documented patterns of lynching and terror; segregated schooling and public facilities |
| 20th-c. welfare/urban policy & labor markets | Institutional exclusion from wealth-building, residential segregation, disabled labor markets | Federal housing policy (FHA), redlining maps, banking/lending institutions, employment networks, unions (often exclusionary) | Color-coded citizenship in policy; risk-models used to justify exclusion | Redlining and discriminatory mortgage denial; racial wealth gap tied to intergenerational homeownership patterns |
| Late 20th–→ 21st-c. carceral/“colorblind” era | Racial control: mass incarceration, surveillance, “neutral” algorithms that reproduce bias; privatized punishment | Police forces, court systems, prisons, parole systems, private-sector algorithms, hiring & credit-scoring systems | Colorblind ideology and “public safety” frame that masks systemic bias; risk assessment language | Disparate sentencing laws and outcomes (e.g., War-on-Drugs impacts); rise of for-profit prisons; algorithmic bias in hiring/credit/insurance |
| Everyday institutions & services (cross-era continuity) | Market-and-service discrimination producing cumulative disadvantage (health, insurance, emergency-response) | Hospitals, insurers, banks, schools, retail, municipal services | Implicit racial valuation treated as market rationality or bureaucratic neutrality | Higher Black maternal mortality; insurance pricing correlated with neighborhood race; worse emergency-response and service outcomes in marginalized neighborhoods |
| Cultural/epistemic domain (continuous) | Curriculum exclusion and narrative erasure; appropriation of African achievements; media stereotyping | Schools (K–PhD), universities, museums, media industries, scholarly canon | Eurocentrism presented as universalism; cultural-denigration of African civilizations | Persistent underrepresentation of African history in curricula; appropriation of Kmt/Nile-Valley symbols without credits; pervasive media stereotyping of Blackness |

---

Across these eras the same methodological logic recurs:
(1) dehumanize or other the targeted population;
(2) legalize or bureaucratize that status so it becomes normal policy;
(3) embed the approach in economic practices that extract wealth or labor;
(4) naturalize the hierarchy through academic, religious or cultural knowledge productions;
(5) renew the instruments (laws → Black Codes → Jim Crow → mass incarceration → market records → algorithmic scoring)
